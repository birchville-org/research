# OIT vs. AMT: Monthly Report September 2026

::: note-box [Executive Summary]
No peer-reviewed study published in September 2026 conclusively settles the OIT vs. AMT dispute. Key insights emerged from a population genetic preprint on Punjabis, evidence for Indus-Mesopotamia maritime connectivity in Syria, and ancient mitogenome analyses from Kashmir.
:::

## 1. Disciplinary Evidence Matrix

| Research Domain | Observed Dataset | Implication for AMT | Implication for OIT | Confidence |
|:----------------|:-----------------|:--------------------|:--------------------|:-----------|
| **Population Genetics** | Zenodo preprint on Punjabi genetic history | Corroborates multi-tier admixture (AASI + IVC + Steppe_MLBA). | Contradicts single-origin autochthonous models. | Preliminary (Preprint) |
| **Near Eastern Contact** | Tooth samples from Tell Ashara/Syria carrying M haplogroups | Confirms human mobility along Indian Ocean / Euphrates networks. | Does not demonstrate Indo-European language spread. | Preliminary (Conference report) |
| **Paleogenetics (Kashmir)** | Burzahom ancient mitogenomes (Neolithic to Medieval) | Highlights localized continuity alongside macro-regional trade. | No evidence for Out-of-India gene flow into the Steppe. | High (Peer-Reviewed) |

## 2. Key Observations

The data in September 2026 continues to demonstrate the asymmetry of the debate: Ancient DNA confirms multi-wave genetic stratification in South Asia, while neither ancient Near Eastern contact nor regional continuity provides evidence for an Out-of-India demographic expansion into Eurasia.
