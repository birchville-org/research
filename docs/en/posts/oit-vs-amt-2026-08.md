# OIT vs. AMT: Monthly Report August 2026

::: note-box [Executive Summary]
The primary peer-reviewed highlight is the *Science Advances* paper by Patterson et al. on ancient Ladakh genomes, establishing a Himalayan interaction corridor. Simultaneously, South Indian universities expanded sampling across Iron Age Tamil Nadu necropolises.
:::

## 1. Disciplinary Evidence Matrix

| Research Domain | Observed Dataset | Implication for AMT | Implication for OIT | Confidence |
|:----------------|:-----------------|:--------------------|:--------------------|:-----------|
| **Archaeogenetics (Ladakh)** | 10 genomes from Old Lady Spider Cave (*Science Advances*) | Highlights high-altitude contact corridors around 800 BCE. | No evidence for Out-of-India homeland models. | Very High (Peer-Reviewed) |
| **Archaeogenetics (South India)** | Preliminary data from Keeladi & Adichanallur | Clarifies Dravidian AASI/IVC demographic foundation in the South. | Neutral / No Steppe affinity. | Preliminary (University release) |
| **Archaeology (Ganges Plain)** | Skeletal sampling at Rajghat (Varanasi) | Crucial for tracking post-Harappan settlement horizons in the Doab. | Open; laboratory analyses pending. | Ongoing Project |

## 2. Synthesis

Mainstream research rejects crude notions of catastrophic conquest, viewing the gradual ingress of Steppe pastoralist ancestry into northwestern South Asia post-1800 BCE as the most robust model for the genetic and linguistic landscape.
