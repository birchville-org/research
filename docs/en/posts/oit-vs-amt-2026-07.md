## 2. Key Findings & Detailed Analysis

### A. Reconstruction of the Genetic History of the Sindhis
Devnani, Gautam, Rawal & Chaubey (*Human Genetics*, DOI: 10.1007/s00439-026-02851-4) integrate modern genome data from 113 Sindhis with reference datasets of ancient DNA.
::: grammar-box
**Result:** The genetic signature of the Sindhi population reproduces the established multi-component model: a dominant early Iranian/indigenous Indus component combined with a significant Steppe input. This supports dynamic immigration models along the Indus Basin.
:::

### B. Rakhigarhi Sample Pipeline
The ASI has consolidated the excavation results of the 2025/26 campaign in Mound 7. The skeletons have been handed over to AnSI, BSIP, and BHU for aDNA, isotope, and facial reconstruction analyses.

Monthly Research (July 2026): Out of India Theory
(OIT) vs. Aryan Invasion/Migration Theory (AIT/AMT)
Executive Summary (Key Statements)
In the reporting month, no major new aDNA datasets on the Bronze Age South Asia/Steppe dominated the major journals; the most important impulses came from (i) a
new population genetic peer-reviewed study on the Sindhi diaspora and (ii) several
reports on new samples/project progress in Rakhigarhi. (PubMed, DD News)
The new Sindhi study (Human Genetics, 2026) models Sindhi genealogy as a
multi-component mixture with a dominant "Indus Periphery"-related component
(~60–66%) plus Steppe-related and AASI-related components; it is thus more
consistent with AMT/"Steppe-Pulse" models (for Northwest India) than with strong OIT
variants, but its statement remains limited to the "Sindhi" population. (PubMed)
Archaeologically/administratively, Rakhigarhi is currently primarily visible as a sampling and
infrastructure project: ASI handed over new skeletal remains (Burials Mound 7) to AnSI;
aDNA, isotope, and osteological analyses are planned in cooperation with, among others,
UCL/BSIP/BHU. Results are still pending, but potentially relevant for the debate,
because additional Harappan aDNA reduces model uncertainty (keyword: previously
extremely low yield). (DD News)
In public reception, the debate remains highly politicized (videos/blogs/Reddit);
methodologically central points (dating, sampling bias, model assumptions in
ADMIXTURE/qpAdm/LAI, language tree vs. populations) are often abbreviated there. For the
report, popular contributions were only considered as an indicator of reception. (THE WEEK
Magazine)

1) New Studies & Finds (with DOI/URL)
1.1 Archaeogenetics / Population Genetics
(A) Peer-Review: Sindhi Diaspora and Northwest South Asia
Devnani, Gautam, Rawal, Chaubey (2026): Blending borders: reconstructing the genetic history
of the Sindhi population (Hum Genet). DOI: 10.1007/s00439-026-02851-4. (PubMed)
Relevance for OIT vs. AMT: The study is not a direct aDNA study of the Bronze Age, but
it integrates modern genome data (113 Indian Sindhis) with reference data including
"ancient DNA samples" for modeling components (Indus Periphery-related,
Steppe-related, AASI-related). (PubMed)
Important for the debate: It reproduces the established picture (since 2019/2020) that
Steppe-related components in parts of Northwest South Asia can be statistically robustly modeled,
without, however, forcing a specific linguistic assignment. (PubMed)

(B) Project/Sample News: Rakhigarhi (new skeletons, announced analyses)
DD News (2026-07-02): ASI transfers skeletal remains from Rakhigarhi to AnSI; announced
are aDNA analyses, stable isotopes, osteological/paleopathological analyses and
environmental reconstruction; collaborations are mentioned with UCL, BSIP, BHU, among others. (DD News)
Debate relevance: Additional Harappan aDNA is central, as previous Harappan
genomes are rare and interpretations (e.g., "no Steppe component in the Harappan
core") depend heavily on the small sample. (DD News)

1.2 Archaeology, Dating, Material Culture
For July 2026, no new peer-reviewed dating revolutions (Sintashta/Andronovo/BMAC/IVC upheavals)
that would immediately re-decide the OIT-vs.-AMT core question were identified in the found, reliably citable sources.
However, institutional activities (ongoing excavations, sample logistics) are visible,
which could provide more directly datable material in the medium to long term. (DD News)

1.3 Linguistics / Philology / Indology
In the search window (last 30 days), no clearly identified new, high-ranking
specialized publications were discovered that would substantially overturn central linguistic standard arguments (e.g., relative
chronology of Indo-Iranian, loanword layers, substrate vocabulary, Rigveda dating).
In the public sphere, known positions (Talageri/Elst vs.
Witzel/Bryant/Mallory/Anthony/Parpola) continue to be negotiated as cultural battle topoi; for
the research situation, these contributions are predominantly secondary.

2) State of the Debate (brief, fair to both positions)
2.1 What "serious consensus" typically means
In genetics and archaeology, a common working model (AMT variant) is that
Steppe_MLBA-related genetic signals are detectable in parts of South Asia (especially North/Northwest),
while Harappan/IVC core populations are modeled more strongly as a mixture of "Iran Neolithic-related" and AASI-related components, depending on the sample/proxy; the exact temporal and spatial fine resolution depends heavily on
aDNA availability. (Context: new Sindhi study as a modern replication of this
component logic.) (PubMed)

2.2 What OIT (in its strong form) typically claims
Strong OIT variants postulate a primary origin scenario of the Indo-Aryans/Indo-Iranians (partly of the Indo-Europeans) in South Asia, with later emigration waves to
Central Asia/Europe; frequently, Vedic chronologies, Sarasvati/"Out of Sarasvati"
narratives, and selective genetic arguments (e.g., R1a diversity) are invoked.

From the perspective of mainstream research, strong OIT variants are considered difficult to reconcile
with the combined evidence from historical linguistics (tree/isoglosses),
archaeology (Steppe Bronze Age complexes), and population genetics; however, it remains
legitimate to empirically test individual sub-hypotheses (e.g., complex internal migrations in South Asia, earlier
contacts).

2.3 What AMT/AIT (today mostly "Migration" instead of "Invasion") claims
AMT models combine: (i) linguistic reconstructions (Indo-Iranian/Indo-Aryan
as a branch), (ii) archaeological corridors (Steppe → Central Asia/BMAC/Inner Asian
Mountain Corridor → South Asia), and (iii) genetic patterns (Steppe-related components in
many northern/northwestern groups).
New this month: The Sindhi study does not provide direct migration dating for the Bronze Age,
but supports the "multi-component" finding and reports estimated
admixture time windows in the 1st millennium BCE for the studied population. (PubMed)

3) Methodological Assessment (why many "updates" do not decide the core question)
1. Sampling problem (aDNA): For Harappan/IVC, the number of published, well-dated
genomes remains low; each additional individual can shift models,
which is why institutional sampling programs (as currently Rakhigarhi/AnSI) are scientifically
significant, but only with data publication. (DD News)
2. Model dependence: Components (Indus Periphery, Steppe, AASI) are analytical
constructs; their sizes and datings depend on references, statistics
(qpAdm/Chromopainter/LAI), and assumptions. The Sindhi study explicitly works with
allele frequency and haplotype methods as well as RoH/IBD/LAI, which is considered standard in the field;
nevertheless, interpretations are always population-specific. (PubMed)
3. Language-Genetics Coupling: Neither OIT nor AMT can be strictly derived from genetics alone;
language change can occur with or without large demography. Therefore,
interdisciplinary triangulation (Linguistics + Archaeology + Genetics) is crucial.
4. Public Debate: Media contributions tend towards "proof" narratives; serious research
communicates uncertainties (confidences, alternative models). The discrepancy is part
of the political reception, not the data situation.

4) Open Questions / What to particularly observe in the coming months
Rakhigarhi aDNA: Will genome-wide data actually be published from the newly transferred individuals (Mound 7, Burials)?
In what quality/coverage and with what direct datings? (DD News)
NW-India / Swat / Gandhara Grave / Late Bronze Age tranches: Will new
datasets emerge that map transitions between IVC periphery, BMAC/IAMC, and Steppe_MLBA in
finer time slices?
Linguistic Chronology: Are there new, methodologically robust works on the relative
dating of Vedic texts (e.g., Bayesian phylogenetics vs. classical philology) and on
substrate lexica?
