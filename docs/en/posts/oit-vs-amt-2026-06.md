# OIT vs. AMT: Monthly Report June 2026

::: note-box [Executive Summary]
In the reporting month of June 2026, announcements of new excavations and sample transfers in the Harappan context predominated over newly published aDNA datasets. The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) reported the recovery of eight skeletons from the Rakhigarhi necropolis, which were transferred to the BSIP in Lucknow.

## 2. Key Findings & Detailed Analysis

### A. Excavations in Rakhigarhi (2025/26 Campaign)
The ASI has completed the excavation of eight burials in Rakhigarhi. Numerous ceramic vessels as grave goods allow for a precise typological classification into the mature to late Harappan phase.
::: grammar-box
**Significance for the Debate:** In the short term, no new sequencing data is yet available. In the medium to long term, however, the sample pipeline is crucial, as the current genetic picture of the Harappan civilization is based on extremely few individuals.
:::

### B. Politicization vs. Scientific Methodology
In public Indian reception, the topic remains highly charged. Scientifically relevant, however, remains solely the question of whether the samples yield evaluable aDNA despite the unfavorable climatic conditions in the alluvium, and how transparently the sequences are published.

**Monthly Research: Out of India vs. Aryan Invasion/Migration ---
June 2026**

**Executive Summary**

- In the reporting month, **announcements of new analyses** (especially
  Harappa/Indus context Rakhigarhi) predominated over newly
  published aDNA datasets: Several Rakhigarhi skeletons were transferred for
  aDNA, isotope, and osteological analyses, as well as
  facial reconstruction, to Indian and international partners
  ([[The New Indian
  Express]{.underline}](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Jun/22/ancient-rakhigarhi-remains-to-reveal-harappan-secrets-through-dna-and-facial-reconstruction)).

- The ASI reports new burial finds in Rakhigarhi (including eight
  skeletons, numerous ceramic grave goods) and states an expected
  analysis report within 2–3 months; samples were sent, among others, to the BSIP
  (Lucknow) for DNA extraction ([[The
  Tribune]{.underline}](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/top-headlines/asi-unearths-8-harappan-era-skeletons-pottery-offerings-at-rakhigarhi/)).

- For the core question OIT vs. AIT/AMT, this means: **no
  new, peer-reviewed "decisive" genetics in the short term**, but potentially
  relevant data pipeline (Harappan burials) --- at the same time, the
  established state of research (Harappa/IVC without
  Steppe component in very few aDNA cases so far; Steppe-related
  admixture mainly from the Bronze/Iron Age in Northwest and in present-day
  populations) remains the central reference framework.

- In public reception, strong politicization remains visible; the academic
  added value currently lies less in opinion pieces than in the question of whether
  the announced samples actually yield high-quality aDNA and how transparently
  the data will be published.

**New Studies & Finds (last ~30 days)**

**1) Rakhigarhi: Transfer of Skeletal Material for
aDNA/Isotopes/Facial Reconstruction (Announcement)**

- **What's new?** Several "nearly 5,000-year-old" human remains
  from Rakhigarhi were transferred for detailed investigations (incl. ancient
  DNA, stable isotopes, osteology, paleopathology,
  environmental reconstruction) as well as facial reconstruction ([[The
  New Indian
  Express]{.underline}](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Jun/22/ancient-rakhigarhi-remains-to-reveal-harappan-secrets-through-dna-and-facial-reconstruction)).

- **Institutions/Collaborations:** ASI (Excavation Branch-II, Greater
  Noida) and AnSI (Kolkata) as well as mentioned partners/references such as BSIP
  Lucknow, University College London (UCL), and Banaras Hindu University
  (BHU) ([[The New Indian
  Express]{.underline}](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Jun/22/ancient-rakhigarhi-remains-to-reveal-harappan-secrets-through-dna-and-facial-reconstruction)).

- **Interpretation for the Debate:** In the OIT/AIT argumentation,
  Rakhigarhi is often treated as a "test case"; here it is important that the
  article **does not report new results**, but rather a
  **research announcement**.

**2) Rakhigarhi: New Burial Finds, Sample Shipment for DNA,
Expected Report**

- **What's new?** ASI reports eight skeletons from a mature
  Harappan burial (Mound No. 7; cemetery area), along with rich
  grave goods (including ~40 ceramic offerings per burial, shell bracelets,
  gold beads) ([[The
  Tribune]{.underline}](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/top-headlines/asi-unearths-8-harappan-era-skeletons-pottery-offerings-at-rakhigarhi/)).

- **Dating/Stratigraphy:** Classified as mature Harappan phase; roughly
  ~4,000–5,000 years before present ([[The
  Tribune]{.underline}](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/top-headlines/asi-unearths-8-harappan-era-skeletons-pottery-offerings-at-rakhigarhi/)).

- **DNA/Analytical Workflow:** Samples from one skeleton were sent to the
  Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences (BSIP), Lucknow, for
  DNA extraction and analysis; three skeletons went to the
  Anthropological Survey of India (AnSI) in Kolkata ([[The
  Tribune]{.underline}](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/top-headlines/asi-unearths-8-harappan-era-skeletons-pottery-offerings-at-rakhigarhi/)).

- **Timeline:** The article mentions an expected submission of the
  analysis report within "two to three months" ([[The
  Tribune]{.underline}](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/top-headlines/asi-unearths-8-harappan-era-skeletons-pottery-offerings-at-rakhigarhi/)).

**State of the Debate (Brief Situation June 2026)**

- **AIT/AMT Side (Mainstream in Archaeogenetics & Historical
  Linguistics):** New primary data for South Asia remains scarce;
  therefore, the established synthetic position (Harappa/IVC without
  Steppe component in very few aDNA cases so far; Steppe-related
  admixture mainly from the Bronze/Iron Age in Northwest and in present-day
  populations) remains the reference until new, reproducible datasets from
  South Asia emerge.

- **OIT Side:** The reporting month primarily provides
  "pipeline" news (Rakhigarhi samples/analyses), which are often framed in OIT-related
  discourses as an impending "game changer";
  methodologically, it is crucial that only **peer-reviewed sequence data**
  (with laboratory protocols, contamination checks, radiocarbon dates, and
  open metadata) gain argumentative relevance.

**Methodological Assessment (what is robust?)**

- **High Value:** Newly sampled burials from Rakhigarhi are
  fundamentally relevant because Harappa/IVC is considered a core context for the question
  "Pre- or Post-Bronze Age Steppe Signals?"; but the
  significance depends entirely on aDNA success, sample number,
  datings, and publication practice.

- **Not yet robust:** Media reports that merely report
  sample transfers or laboratory starts are **provisional** for OIT vs. AMT
  argumentatively and cannot shift consensus
  as long as data is missing.

- **Known Structural Hurdle:** South Asian aDNA is very difficult to obtain due to
  climate/preservation; individual genomes can be
  overinterpreted if they are not embedded in a larger dataset.

**Open Questions & Expected Next Steps**

- Will the announced Rakhigarhi samples actually lead to
  **publishable genomes** (including Y-chromosome data), or
  will it remain partial results?

- How will radiocarbon dates, archaeological context
  (mound/layer), and genetic data be integrated in a publication?

- If an analysis report is available in 2–3 months (as reported in media),
  it is unclear if and when a preprint/article will emerge from it.
