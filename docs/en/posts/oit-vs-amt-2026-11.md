# OIT vs. AMT: Monthly Report November 2026

::: note-box [Executive Summary]
During the reporting period of November 2026, two *Nature* studies on the spread of Germanic and Celtic languages refine the aDNA Steppe model, while whole genomes from North Pakistan and a bioRxiv preprint on Zebu domestication sharpen west-to-east dispersal dynamics. The strongest geochronological finding against classical OIT synchronizations is provided by evidence in *Communications Earth & Environment* that the glacier-fed Paleo-Sutlej decoupled from the Ghaggar-Hakra system as early as the Late Pleistocene (> 60–15 ka).

## 2. Key Findings & Detailed Analysis

### A. Indo-European aDNA Syntheses and South Asia-Related Genomics
In late October and early November 2026, McColl, Kroonen et al. published two *Nature* articles using thousands of ancient genomes to show how Germanic (~4000 BP via the Baltics) and Celtic branches (~3200–2800 BP from Central Europe) can be linked to Steppe-derived populations. For South Asia, Gao et al. (*MBE*, November 1, 2026) sharpen the distinction between East Asian agricultural waves (~4,800 and ~1,600 BP) and *Steppe_MLBA* using 115 whole genomes from North Pakistan. Furthermore, Miraszek et al. (bioRxiv, November 6, 2026) demonstrate that Zebu cattle (*Bos indicus*) did not originate independently in the Indus Valley but through admixture of Near Eastern Taurine cattle with Indian aurochs.

### B. Late Pleistocene Paleo-Sutlej Relocation
Vimal Singh, Sampat K. Tandon et al. (*Communications Earth & Environment*, November 7, 2026) provide evidence through OSL dating and the tectonically uplifted *Ropar Surface* that the Paleo-Sutlej decoupled from the Ghaggar-Hakra system between > 60,000 and 15,000 years ago. During the Mature Harappan phase, settlements there were thus dependent on the monsoon. This is confirmed by new 4.2k drought archives from the Northeast Himalayas (Verma et al.) and Haryana (Dutt et al.).

### C. Substrate Proxies, Indus Corpus Critique, and Soma/Haoma
Subba (Research Square, November 24, 2026) distinguishes areal convergence from substrate adoption using ordered comparative proxies, while Gokhale (*South Asian Studies*, November 6, 2026) criticizes decontextualized decipherment claims of the Indus script. On Zenodo, El Ghouili (November 19, 2026) places the Vedic-Avestan Soma/Haoma question in the BMAC context, while Rout (November 22, 2026) and Neogi (November 19, 2026) reflect current Sarasvati and precession debates.

# Monthly Research Report – Out of India Theory (OIT) vs. Aryan Migration Theory (AMT) / Steppe Hypothesis

## November 2026

**Reporting Period:** October 28 – November 27, 2026  
**Date of Creation:** November 27, 2026  
**Research Profile & Methodology:** Evaluation of peer-reviewed articles (Nature, Cell, Molecular Biology and Evolution, Communications Earth & Environment, Quaternary Science Reviews, Palaeo-3, Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports, South Asian Studies, Nepalese Linguistics) and preprints (bioRxiv, Research Square, Zenodo, HAL Open Science) from the last 30 days.

### 1. Executive Summary

- **Archaeogenetics & Paleogenetics:** Two *Nature* studies (McColl, Kroonen et al.) on the spread of Germanic and Celtic languages underpin the paradigm of tracing Indo-European language branches via Steppe ancestry and aDNA gradients. A *Cell* study (Aydoğan et al.) demonstrates sustained mobility and South Caucasus contacts in Chalcolithic Anatolia. For South Asia, Gao et al. (*MBE*) quantify two East Asian gene flow waves (~4,800 and ~1,600 BP) beyond the Steppe influx, using 115 whole genomes from North Pakistan (Burusho, Shina, Hazarewal). On bioRxiv, Miraszek et al. show that Zebu cattle (*Bos indicus*) were not independently domesticated in the Indus Valley but arose from the admixture of Near Eastern Taurine cattle with Indian aurochs. No published aDNA data are yet available for the Rakhigarhi skeletons (Mound 7) transferred to AnSI/BSIP; R1a-Z93 and *Steppe_MLBA* remain absent in the Mature Harappan horizon.
- **Archaeology & Paleoclimatology:** Vimal Singh, Sampat K. Tandon et al. (*Communications Earth & Environment*, November 7, 2026) demonstrate, using OSL geochronology along the *Ropar Surface*, that the Paleo-Sutlej was tectonically decoupled from the Ghaggar-Hakra system as early as the Late Pleistocene (> 60–15 ka). Mature Harappan settlements there thus depended on the monsoon, not on a Holocene glacier-fed "Sarasvati" river. Geoarchives from Haryana (Dutt et al.) and the Northeast Himalayas (Verma et al.) confirm the 4.2k drought event as a driver of de-urbanization. Isotopes from Dholavira (Kesarwani et al.) and osteometry from Rupar, Kalibangan, and Lothal (Gautam et al.) show continuity without an autochthonous horse or chariot economy.
- **Historical Linguistics:** Studies by Pokharel, Kashaf & Jabarkhail, and Subba methodologically separate inherited Indo-Aryan structures from areal convergence and non-Indo-European substrates. Gokhale (*South Asian Studies*) rejects purely mathematical-algorithmic decipherment claims of the Indus script and equations with Sanskrit.
- **Indology & Text Philology:** Preprints document, on the one hand, archaeoastronomical early datings (Neogi) and Sarasvati debates (Rout), and on the other hand, El Ghouili's comparison of Soma/Haoma in the BMAC region supports the localization of the shared Indo-Iranian ritual tradition in Central Asia.

### 2. Archaeogenetics & Paleogenetics: Steppe References, North Pakistan, and Cattle Domestication

#### 2.1 Indo-European Comparative Models in Nature and Cell
In late October and early November 2026, the group around Hugh McColl, Guus Kroonen, and Eske Willerslev published two large-scale *Nature* syntheses:
- **Germanic (October 28, 2026):** From 712 new and 4,009 published genomes, the authors identify a Steppe-derived population that migrated from the Baltics to Sweden around 4000 BP as the probable carrier of early Germanic (~800 years later than traditionally dated).
- **Celtic (November 4, 2026):** Based on 4,587 ancient genomes, the Steppe/Bell Beaker arrival (~4400 BP) is followed by a Late Bronze Age westward expansion from eastern Central Europe (Urnfield/Knovíz context, ~3200–2800 BP).
- **Classification:** Both works show that only high-resolution kinship and haplotype analyses can link individual Indo-European sub-branches with Steppe groups. Any genomic correlate for an OIT dispersal from North India to Western Eurasia is missing. In parallel, Aydoğan et al. (*Cell*, November 2026) demonstrate, using 44 genomes from Chalcolithic Anatolia (6000–3000 BCE), 3,000 years of mobility with gene flow from Eastern Anatolia and the South Caucasus, supporting early splits between Anatolian and Steppe branches.

#### 2.2 South Asian Population Genomics and Zebu Origin
- **North Pakistan Genomics (Gao et al., *MBE*, November 1, 2026):** 115 whole genomes (Burusho, Shina, Hazarewal) compared with 2,029 modern and 4,358 ancient genomes clearly separate two East Asian gene flow waves (~4,800 BP demic with rice cultivation along the southern Himalayas; ~1,600 BP from the north) from the Bronze Age *Steppe_MLBA* component.
- **Thar Selection & aDNA Quality Control:** Rathod, Jain, and Mukerji (bioRxiv, November 19, 2026) describe 348 arid adaptation variants in endogamous Thar groups against the backdrop of *Indus-Periphery* continuity. Huang and Ringbauer (bioRxiv, November 20, 2026) introduce `fastpmr`, a >1000-fold faster kinship verification tool for the entire Allen Ancient DNA Resource (AADR).
- **Zebu Domestication (Miraszek et al., bioRxiv, November 6, 2026):** Whole genomes show that *Bos indicus* was not independently domesticated in the Indus Valley but arose from the admixture of Near Eastern Taurine cattle with wild Indian aurochs (238 aurochs and 283 taurine adaptation genes) – a west-to-east Neolithic diffusion trend.

### 3. Archaeology & Paleoclimatology: Paleo-Sutlej, 4.2k Drought, and Subsistence

#### 3.1 Late Pleistocene Sutlej Decoupling from Ghaggar-Hakra
On November 7, 2026, Vimal Singh, Sampat K. Tandon et al. (*Communications Earth & Environment*) refuted a late Holocene decoupling of the Sutlej from the Ghaggar-Hakra system. Mapping, stratigraphy, and OSL dating of the tectonically uplifted *Ropar Surface* prove that the redirection of the southward-flowing Paleo-Sutlej along a transverse structure of the *Main Frontal Thrust* occurred between > 60,000 and 15,000 years ago. Thus, during the Mature Harappan phase (ca. 2600–1900 BCE), no Himalayan glaciers fed the Ghaggar-Hakra basin, but primarily monsoon precipitation. The Rigvedic description of a Sarasvati flowing from the mountains to the sea cannot therefore be geochronologically supported by urban Harappan hydrology.

#### 3.2 Monsoon Dynamics, Animal Husbandry, and Osteometry
- **4.2k Event:** Yachna Verma et al. (*Palaeo-3*, October 31, 2026) document abrupt monsoon fluctuations with a pronounced 4.2k drought signal for 4800–3500 yr BP; Som Dutt et al. (*Quaternary Science Reviews*, November 3, 2026) date the transition to stabilized floodplains with calcretes at the Ghaggar-Yamuna system (Kaithal). Both support climate-driven de-urbanization without invasion.
- **Subsistence & Anthropology:** Manisha Kesarwani et al. (*JAS: Reports*, November 19, 2026) reconstruct local vs. mobile pastoralism using δ13C, δ18O, and 87Sr/86Sr on cattle and buffalo teeth from Dholavira; evidence for domesticated horses (*Equus caballus*) remains absent. Manjulika Gautam et al. (Research Square, November 9, 2026) show uniform stature and robusticity patterns (189.3 ± 20.5 cm; p = 0.21) in 46 skeletons from Rupar, Kalibangan, and Lothal. Additionally, the ERC project AGROCHRONO (Casanova, HAL, November 6, 2026) investigates the spread of agropastoralism from the Zagros to the Indus Valley, while Ramsden (*Asian Culture and History*, November 20, 2026) comparatively analyzes Eurasian Steppe nomads and their mobility technology.

---

### 4. Historical Linguistics: Substrate Proxies and Corpus Critique

#### 4.1 Substrate Evidence vs. Areal Convergence
- **Methodological Synthesis:** Madhav Prasad Pokharel (*Nepalese Linguistics*, November 5, 2026) confronts classical philological migration narratives with archaeogenetics and Bayesian glottochronology. Mohammad Qaseem Kashaf and Azizullah Jabarkhail (*Mankind Adam to Me*, November 22, 2026) summarize the development from Old to New Indo-Aryan as an interplay of Indo-European heritage and long-standing contact with Dravidian, Munda, and unknown substrates.
- **Proxy Tests without Early Evidence:** Nawa Raj Subba (Research Square, November 24, 2026) tests a graded comparison hierarchy (extra-Indian Indo-European languages, Sanskrit, Plains-/Pahari-Indo-Aryan, Shina, Assamese, Tibeto-Burman) on copula and agent markings in Nepali to methodologically separate areal convergence from substrate transfer – relevant for evaluating non-Indo-European relics even in the earliest Vedic.

#### 4.2 Critique of Algorithmic Indus Decipherments
Pallavee Gokhale (*South Asian Studies*, November 6, 2026) criticizes data science approaches that attempt to decipher the Indus script as a mathematical puzzle without archaeological contextualization and prematurely interpret it as Sanskrit or Indo-Aryan.

---

### 5. Indology & Text Philology: Sarasvati, Precession, and Soma/Haoma

#### 5.1 Sarasvati Debate and Archaeoastronomy
Narayan Rout (Zenodo, November 22, 2026) outlines nine open questions regarding the *Naditama*-Sarasvati between the Yamuna and Sutlej in the *Nadistuti-Sukta* (Rigveda 10.75). Given the Late Pleistocene Sutlej diversion (Singh & Tandon 2026), the assumption of a Mature Harappan Sarasvati as a contemporaneous Rigvedic depiction remains geologically unsubstantiated. In parallel, S. Neogi (Zenodo, November 19, 2026) extends the precessional dating of Nakshatra transitions (*Krittika*, *Rohini*, *Mrigashirsha*) to before 4000 BCE – an approach rejected in philological consensus due to retrograde calendar projection and the Rigvedic horse/chariot inventory.

#### 5.2 Soma/Haoma and BMAC Continuity
Karim Adamini El Ghouili (Zenodo, November 19, 2026, v2.0) separates textual findings on Vedic Soma and Avestan Haoma from BMAC archaeology (Gonur, Togolok) and evaluates *Ephedra* spp. over *Peganum harmala* as the most promising botanical candidate. Regardless of open vessel interpretations, the shared Soma/Haoma terminology demonstrates a common Central Asian-Iranian originating tradition, not a unilateral emanation from India.
