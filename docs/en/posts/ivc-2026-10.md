## 2. Key Findings & Detailed Analysis

### A. Mohenjo-Daro: Stratigraphic Revision of the Kot Diji Fortification
A fundamental chronological readjustment is underway at the site of Mohenjo-Daro (Sindh, Pakistan). An international team led by the Directorate General of Antiquities and Archaeology Sindh and Jonathan Mark Kenoyer conducted re-excavations on the western flank of the Stupa Mound, supported by five new AMS-14C series.

::: grammar-box
**Correction of a 70-year-old research error:** The approximately 7-meter thick mudbrick structure uncovered by Sir Mortimer Wheeler in 1950 was then interpreted as a mere flood protection embankment (bund). The stratigraphic sections now unequivocally demonstrate a multi-phase city fortification wall of the pre-classical Kot Diji phase (ca. 3300–2600 BCE). Mohenjo-Daro thus did not emerge suddenly as a new foundation of the Mature Harappan phase (from 2600 BCE), but already possessed a fortified urban precursor in the early 4th millennium BCE.
:::

### B. Regional Craft Centers and Maritime Trade Routes: Lothal and Kutch
In Gujarat, the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) continues large-scale excavations in Lothal (in preparation for the establishment of the National Maritime Heritage Complex, NMHC). Tidal basins and workshops are in focus:
- **Craft Continuity:** Bead-making kilns, raw material depots of carnelian and agate, and cut waste from marine shells (*Turbinella pyrum*) attest to specialized workshop complexes. These were not abruptly abandoned after 1900 BCE but seamlessly transitioned into the regional Sorath-Harappan culture.
- **Sea Routes to Mesopotamia (Meluhha):** Geochemical bitumen analyses and stamp seal finds in the Gulf region (Magan/Oman, Dilmun/Bahrain) confirm a dense maritime network. Outposts like Lakhapar (Kutch) secured raw material routes for semi-precious stones, copper, and ivory to the Mesopotamian cities of the Sargonid and Ur III periods.

### C. Rakhigarhi Mound 7: Laboratory Analyses of the aDNA and Isotope Pipeline
Following the completion of field campaigns at the necropolis of Mound 7 in Rakhigarhi (Haryana), complete skeletons were transferred to the Anthropological Survey of India (AnSI) in Kolkata. Interdisciplinary laboratory analyses are underway in collaboration with the Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences (BSIP) and CCMB Hyderabad:
- **Ultrashort-Fragment aDNA Extraction:** Due to severe chemical hydrolysis in alluvial soil, enzymatic methods are being used for fragments under 35 base pairs. These aim to validate the single genome published in 2019 (individual I6113) on a broader sample base.
- **Isotope Analyses:** Strontium isotope ratios (87Sr/86Sr) and oxygen values (δ18O) in tooth enamel allow for the differentiation of local urban dwellers from migrants from the Balochistan highlands or coastal regions.
- **Population Modeling (qpAdm):** Confirmation of the two-stream model (autochthonous AASI lineage + pre-agrarian Zagros/Iran kinship without steppe or Anatolian admixture in the urban Mature phase).

### D. Epigraphy: NLP Models Against the Non-Script Thesis
Spurred by the 1 million US dollar Iravatham Mahadevan Prize, the application of modern computational linguistics has intensified:
- **Computer Vision & OCR:** Deep-learning models (YOLO, Convolutional Neural Networks) automate grapheme recognition and segmentation on weathered steatite seals and pottery sherds for the corpus of approximately 5,500 inscriptions.
- **Syntactic Regularities & Entropy:** Statistical entropy analyses and n-gram sequences demonstrate fixed positional rules for prefix, core, and suffix signs. This refutes the thesis of a purely non-linguistic symbol system (Farmer/Witzel) and supports classification as a logosyllabic script.
- **Rebus Principle:** In linguistic discourse, the Proto-Dravidian Hypothesis (Asko Parpola) maintains its leading position (e.g., fish sign *mīn* = both "fish" and "star"), although AI-supported readings remain formally unproven without a bilingual text.

### E. Paleoclimate: Centennial Megadroughts and Orderly De-Urbanization
A publication in *Communications Earth & Environment* (IIT Gandhinagar) provided quantitative climate data for the Indus basin:
- **Four Megadroughts:** Speleothem profiles from Dharamjali Cave and lake sediments show four drought phases between 4400 and 3400 BP, each lasting over 85 years.
- **Weakening of the Summer Monsoon:** Around 4260 BP (ca. 2260 BCE), aridification began as part of the global 4.2k climate event, affecting up to 91 percent of the basin. Causes included a southward migrating Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ), intensified El Niño cycles, and negative phases of the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD).
- **Orderly Transformation:** Pollen analyses by BSIP (Deoria Tal, among others) document the drying up of permanent tributaries in the Ghaggar-Hakra system. Agriculture responded adaptively: a shift from winter cereals to summer monsoon-dependent millets and an orderly migration to the wetter Yamuna-Ganges region and Gujarat.


## 3. Synthesis & Conclusions

The findings of the October 2026 reporting month paint a new, nuanced picture of the Indus Culture:
1. **Extended Urbanization Chronology:** Monumental architecture and fortification walls already begin in the early 4th millennium BCE (Kot Diji).
2. **Genetic and Demographic Continuity:** The autochthonous AASI/Iran Neolithic profile remains the empirical reference for the IVC population.
3. **Resilient Transformation:** Instead of an apocalyptic collapse, the civilization responded to centuries of extreme aridification with flexible small-scale agricultural adaptation and eastward migration.

### 1. Executive Summary

During the reporting period of October 2026, interdisciplinary research and excavation projects on the Indus Valley Culture (IVC / Harappan Civilization) yielded crucial advances in four core areas:

1. **Archaeology & Urbanism:** The re-evaluation of monumental mudbrick fortifications in Mohenjo-Daro (Sindh, Pakistan) through AMS-14C dating demonstrates that urban monumental architecture already extends back to the early Kot Diji phase (ca. 3300–2600 BCE). The 7-meter structure at the Stupa Mound, interpreted by Wheeler in 1950 as a flood embankment, is now proven to be a defensive city wall. In parallel, new ASI excavations in the port city of Lothal and in Lakhapar (Kutch) provide evidence for an unbroken continuity of craft and maritime trade with Magan and Dilmun into the Post-Urban period.
2. **Archaeogenetics & Paleoanthropology:** The Anthropological Survey of India (AnSI) and the Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences (BSIP) continue aDNA, isotope, and paleopathology analyses of new finds from the necropolis of Rakhigarhi (Mound 7). Ultrashort-fragment methods address thermal DNA degradation to confirm the genetic model (autochthonous AASI lineage + pre-agrarian Zagros/Iran kinship without a Steppe component in the Mature phase) and to elucidate mobility patterns via strontium isotopes (87Sr/86Sr).
3. **Epigraphy & AI-powered Script Research:** The Iravatham Mahadevan Prize accelerated the use of Computer Vision (YOLO/CNN-OCR) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) for the analysis of the approximately 5,500 inscriptions in the Indus corpus. Statistical entropy and n-gram models demonstrate syntactic regularities of a logosyllabic writing system and refute purely symbolic theses. In linguistic classification, the refinement of Proto-Dravidian rebus models dominates over isolated substratum hypotheses.
4. **Paleoclimatology & De-Urbanization:** Speleothem data from Dharamjali Cave and lake sediment cores document four centennial megadroughts (> 85 years duration) in the 4400–3400 BP timeframe. The 4.2k event significantly weakened the summer monsoon. The drying up of perennial rivers in the Ghaggar-Hakra system did not lead to an abrupt collapse but to an orderly de-urbanization: a shift from winter cereals to summer monsoon-dependent millets and an eastward relocation to the wetter Yamuna-Ganges basin.

### 2. Archaeology, Urbanism & Settlement Studies

#### 2.1 Mohenjo-Daro: Stratigraphic Revision of the Kot Diji Fortification
A fundamental chronological readjustment is underway at the site of Mohenjo-Daro (Sindh, Pakistan). An international team led by the Directorate General of Antiquities and Archaeology Sindh and Jonathan Mark Kenoyer conducted re-excavations on the western flank of the Stupa Mound, supported by five new AMS-14C series.

The findings revise Sir Mortimer Wheeler's interpretation from 1950. Wheeler interpreted an approximately 7-meter thick mudbrick structure as a mere flood protection embankment (bund). The stratigraphic sections now unequivocally demonstrate a multi-phase city fortification wall. Its basal layers belong to the Kot Diji phase (ca. 3300–2600 BCE). Mohenjo-Daro thus did not emerge suddenly as a new foundation of the Mature Harappan phase (from 2600 BCE), but already possessed a fortified urban precursor in the early 4th millennium BCE.

#### 2.2 Maritime Trade Routes and Craft Centers: Lothal and Kutch
In Gujarat, the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) continues large-scale excavations in Lothal. Accompanying the establishment of the National Maritime Heritage Complex (NMHC), tidal basins and workshops are in focus:
- **Craft Continuity:** Bead-making kilns, raw material depots of carnelian and agate, and cut waste from marine shells (Turbinella pyrum) attest to specialized workshop complexes. These were not abruptly abandoned after 1900 BCE but seamlessly transitioned into the regional Sorath-Harappan culture.
- **Sea Routes to Mesopotamia (Meluhha):** Geochemical bitumen analyses and stamp seal finds in the Gulf region (Magan/Oman, Dilmun/Bahrain) confirm a dense maritime network. Outposts like Lakhapar (Kutch) secured raw material routes for semi-precious stones, copper, and ivory to the Mesopotamian cities of the Sargonid and Ur III periods.

### 3. Archaeogenetics & Paleoanthropology

#### 3.1 The Rakhigarhi Mound 7 Research Program
Following the completion of field campaigns at the necropolis of Mound 7 in Rakhigarhi (Haryana), complete skeletons were transferred to the Anthropological Survey of India (AnSI) in Kolkata. Interdisciplinary laboratory analyses are underway in collaboration with the Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences (BSIP) and CCMB Hyderabad:
- **Ultrashort-Fragment aDNA Extraction:** Due to severe chemical hydrolysis in alluvial soil, enzymatic methods are being used for fragments under 35 base pairs. These aim to validate the single genome published in 2019 (individual I6113) on a broader sample base.
- **Isotope Analyses:** Strontium isotope ratios (87Sr/86Sr) and oxygen values (δ18O) in tooth enamel allow for the differentiation of local urban dwellers from migrants from the Balochistan highlands or coastal regions.
- **Paleopathology:** Microscopic findings of porotic hyperostosis, cribra orbitalia, and dental caries document infection pressure and dietary changes in the Late Urban period.

#### 3.2 Population Genetic Modeling (qpAdm)
Genomic analyses via qpAdm confirm the two-component population model of the Harappan period:
1. **Autochthonous Component (AASI):** A basal South Asian hunter-gatherer lineage (Ancient Ancestral South Indian).
2. **Iran Neolithic Lineage:** A West Asian component that diverged from the pastoralist lineages of the Zagros Mountains before the onset of agriculture.
3. **Absence of Steppe and Anatolian DNA:** For the urban Mature phase, the absence of Pontic-Caspian Steppe DNA (Yamnaya) and Anatolian early farmer signatures remains characteristic. Gene flow from the Central Asian steppes occurred only in the 2nd millennium BCE during the transition to the post-urban phase.

### 4. Epigraphy, Script Research & Linguistic Hypotheses

#### 4.1 Digital Corpus Acquisition and NLP Models
Spurred by the 1 million US dollar Iravatham Mahadevan Prize, the application of modern computational linguistics has intensified:
- **Computer Vision & OCR:** Deep-learning models (YOLO, Convolutional Neural Networks) automate grapheme recognition and segmentation on weathered steatite seals and pottery sherds. This creates a cleaned data basis for the corpus of approximately 5,500 inscriptions.
- **Syntactic Regularities & Entropy:** Statistical entropy analyses and n-gram sequences demonstrate fixed positional rules for prefix, core, and suffix signs. This refutes the thesis of a purely non-linguistic symbol system (Farmer/Witzel) and supports classification as a logosyllabic script.

#### 4.2 Linguistic Hypotheses and Rebus Principle
In linguistic discourse, the Proto-Dravidian Hypothesis (Asko Parpola) maintains its leading position:
- **Rebus Principle:** Pictorial signs are interpreted via homophony (e.g., the fish sign *mīn* = both "fish" and "star" in Dravidian), yielding plausible astronomical and administrative titles. Parallels to megalithic pottery graffiti in South India support this classification.
- **Validation Limit:** Without a bilingual text (analogous to the Rosetta Stone) and with an extremely short average length of only 4.6 characters per inscription, AI-supported readings remain formally unproven.

### 5. Paleoclimatology, Environmental History & De-Urbanization

#### 5.1 Centennial Droughts and the 4.2k Event
A publication in *Communications Earth & Environment* (IIT Gandhinagar) provided quantitative climate data for the Indus basin:
- **Four Megadroughts:** Speleothem profiles from Dharamjali Cave and lake sediments show four drought phases between 4400 and 3400 BP, each lasting over 85 years.
- **Weakening of the Summer Monsoon:** Around 4260 BP (ca. 2260 BCE), aridification began as part of the global 4.2k climate event, affecting up to 91 percent of the basin. Causes included a southward migrating Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ), intensified El Niño cycles, and negative phases of the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD).

#### 5.2 Transformation of the Ghaggar-Hakra River System
Pollen analyses by BSIP (including from Deoria Tal) show a decline in moisture-loving tree pollen around 4250 cal yr BP, documenting the drying up of permanent tributaries in the Ghaggar-Hakra system.

De-urbanization proceeded in an orderly and adaptive manner:
- **Agricultural Shift:** Agriculture responded to weaker winter rains by increasing the cultivation of summer monsoon-dependent, drought-resistant millets (such as pearl and foxtail millet) instead of wheat and barley.
- **Eastward Settlement Shift:** Due to a lack of river water for large urban infrastructures, populations retreated from the western metropolises and shifted their settlement centers to the wetter Yamuna-Ganges basin and Gujarat (Late and Post-Harappan horizons such as Cemetery H and OCP).
