## 2. Key Findings & Detailed Analysis

### A. Lactase Persistence: The Molecular Marker of Steppe Admixture
The population genetic reconstruction by Kerdoncuff et al. closes a crucial gap between dietary evolution and migration.
::: grammar-box
**Core Evidence of Lactase Genetics:** The Eurasian lactase persistence allele (-13.910:C>T) did not arrive in South Asia before 1800–1500 BCE. Its regional concentration directly correlates with the qpAdm model value for Steppe_MLBA. The allele is completely absent in the mature Indus genome (Rakhigarhi I6113). This refutes models of an Out-of-India expansion of pastoralist societies.
:::

### B. Sinauli: Solid-Disk Wheels Refute the Chariot Narrative
The permanent exhibition of the finds from Sinauli (Baghpat, UP) at the National Museum Delhi once again brought the technical construction of the grave goods into focus:
- **Massive Three-Part Wheels:** The wheels consist of three massive wooden planks with copper fittings (solid-disk wheels). They have no spokes.
- **Absence of Horses:** Neither *Equus caballus* bones nor horse harness equipment are detectable in the find complex. The yokes are dimensioned for draft cattle.

### C. Historical Linguistics: Wheel Vocabulary and Rigvedic Substrate
The debate between phylolinguistic tree modeling (Heggarty et al.) and Indo-Europeanist paleontology shows:
- The common reconstruction of technical terms such as *kwekwlo- ("wheel") and *yug-om ("yoke") presupposes a split after the global emergence of wheeled vehicles (ca. 3500 BCE).
- The Rigvedic vocabulary contains 300–400 loanwords from Para-Dravidian, Para-Munda, and an unknown substrate language ("Language X"), which argues against an autochthonous origin of Sanskrit in the Indus Valley.

# Monthly Research Report – Out of India Theory (OIT) vs. Aryan Migration Theory (AMT) / Steppe Hypothesis

## October 2026

**Reporting Period:** October 1 – October 30, 2026
**Date of Creation:** October 30, 2026
**Research Profile & Methodology:** Multidisciplinary evaluation of aDNA primary data, current preprints (bioRxiv, medRxiv, Zenodo, arXiv) and peer-reviewed publications (Nature Portfolio, Science, Cell, PNAS, Journal of Human Genetics), as well as official academic excavation reports (Archaeological Survey of India [ASI], Anthropological Survey of India [AnSI], Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences [BSIP]). ### 1. Executive Summary

- **Archaeogenetics & Population Genetics:** In the October 2026 reporting window, the population genetic consensus of the Steppe Migration (AMT) is strengthened by new methodological analyses. A large-scale study on lactase persistence in South Asia (Kerdoncuff et al.) demonstrates that the dominant Eurasian allele (-13.910:C>T) was introduced into the subcontinent only post-Harappan by Steppe pastoralists (Steppe_MLBA); its current frequency distribution directly correlates with the proportion of Steppe ancestry and not with regional in-situ selection. Simultaneously, the multidisciplinary laboratory pipeline for eight newly excavated skeletons from Rakhigarhi Mound 7 (AnSI Kolkata and BSIP Lucknow) has been formally initiated. Sequence data are still pending, but the absence of Steppe and R1a-Z93 lineages in the urban Harappan horizon (ca. 2600–1900 BCE) remains the empirical standard. Genetic evidence for an emigration from India ("Out of India") to Central or West Asia still does not exist.
- **Archaeology & Paleoclimatology:** The debate surrounding the Bronze Age finds from Sinauli (Baghpat, UP) flared up again with the museum presentation of the 4000-year-old carts at the National Museum New Delhi. The archaeological evidence clearly argues against true chariots with spoked wheels: these are massive solid-disk wheeled carts, and no horse bones (*Equus caballus*) are found at the site. The traction devices morphologically indicate draft cattle (*Bos indicus*). Concurrently, new high-resolution speleothem data from Dharamjali Cave and lake sediment cores from BSIP document four centennial megadroughts (> 85 years duration) during the global 4.2k climate event. This aridification explains the ordered de-urbanization and eastward shift of Harappan settlements into the Ganges-Yamuna Doab without military foreign intervention.
- **Historical Linguistics:** The scholarly discussion about the computational "hybrid model" by Heggarty et al. versus the classical Steppe model (Lazaridis et al.) highlights the methodological limitations of pure phylogenetic tree algorithms. Indo-Europeanists emphasize the "archaeolinguistic anachronism": a root dating around 6000 BCE irreconcilably conflicts with the pan-Indo-European shared vocabulary for wheeled vehicles (*kwekwlo-), which archaeologically only appears around 3500 BCE. In the domain of Old Indo-Aryan (Rigveda), substrate analyses confirm a complex mosaic of Para-Dravidian, Para-Munda, and an unidentified substrate language ("Language X"). Mitanni-Aryan (ca. 1400 BCE) continues to serve as an irrefutable *terminus ante quem* for the separation of the Indo-Aryan from the Iranian dialects.
- **Indology & Text Philology:** Text-critical philology (Witzel, Jamison) again rejected attempts to predate the Rigveda to the 4th or 3rd millennium BCE through archaeoastronomical retro-calculations (Kak, Frawley). The mythological metaphors of the text do not allow for astronomical precision datings. The Nadistuti-Sukta (RV 10.75) documents a strict geographical order from east to west (Ganga, Yamuna, Sarasvati, Shutudri), proving that the late Rigveda located the Sarasvati in the degenerated Ghaggar-Hakra riverbed, synchronous with the post-urban time horizon of the late 2nd millennium BCE.

### 2. Archaeogenetics & Paleogenetics: Lactase Persistence and Harappan Pipelines

#### 2.1 Lactase Persistence as a Demographic Tracer of Steppe Admixture
A significant contribution to South Asian population history was provided by the publication of Kerdoncuff et al. on the evolution of lactase persistence (LP) based on over 8,000 modern and ancient genomes.
- **The Allele -13.910:C>T:** The dominant Eurasian mutation for adult lactase tolerance (-13.910:C>T) shows frequencies between 20% and 35% in Northwest India and Pakistan, but rapidly drops to below 5% in South and East India.
- **Coupling to Steppe_MLBA:** Using formal F-statistics (*f4*, *qpAdm*), the authors demonstrated that the frequency of this allele directly correlates with the proportion of Steppe ancestry (Steppe_MLBA, associated with Sintashta and Andronovo complexes). In contrast to European populations, where strong positive selection pressure is demonstrable, in most South Asian groups, pure demographic admixture explains the allele frequency.
- **Significance for OIT Refutation:** In pre-Steppe aDNA samples from the subcontinent (Rakhigarhi I6113 as well as IVC-associated migrants from Shahr-i Sokhta and Gonur), the allele -13.910:C>T is completely absent. If dairy farming with lactase persistence had been an autochthonous product of the Indus Civilization that spread to Eurasia (as postulated in OIT models), this allele would have to be detectable in the mature Harappan genome. Its late appearance synchronous with Steppe genomic components (after 1800–1500 BCE) clearly demonstrates gene flow from North to South.

#### 2.2 Status of aDNA Investigations at Rakhigarhi Mound 7
The skeletons excavated by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) during the 2025/2026 excavation season in Rakhigarhi Mound 7 (cemetery area) were institutionally transferred during the reporting period.
- **Cooperation:** The Anthropological Survey of India (AnSI, Kolkata) and the Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences (BSIP, Lucknow) are conducting joint sampling for aDNA, collagen radiocarbon dating, and strontium (87Sr/86Sr) and oxygen isotope (delta-18O) analysis.
- **Findings:** Reliable aDNA sequences or Y-chromosomal haplogroup determinations have not yet been published. Claims in social media about alleged R1a or Steppe finds in these new skeletons lack any data basis. Until the appearance of peer-reviewed results, the findings of Shinde et al. (Cell 2019) remain authoritative: the IVC basal population consisted of AASI and Iran Neolithic-related ancestry without a Steppe component.

### 3. Archaeology & Paleoclimatology: Sinauli Carts, Horse Finds, and Megadroughts

#### 3.1 The Sinauli Carts and the Chariot Narrative
In October, the Sinauli site (Baghpat District, Uttar Pradesh; dated ca. 2000–1800 BCE, transition from late Harappan to OCP/Copper-Hoard horizons) once again became the focus of archaeological debate after the reconstructions were permanently exhibited at the National Museum New Delhi.
- **Wheel Technology:** The detailed technological examination of the carts confirms three-part solid-disk wheels, fitted with copper triangles. No spoked wheels are found. True light chariots, however, require spoked wheels to be agile and maneuverable in combat.
- **Faunal Association:** No horse bones (*Equus caballus*) or metallic bit cheekpieces were recovered in the entire excavation complex of Sinauli. The yoke constructions correspond to those for draft cattle (*Bos indicus*).
- **Assessment:** Sinauli provides evidence of a highly developed indigenous warrior elite in the Doab with elaborate coffins and copper helmets, but it does not support the hypothesis of an autochthonous Indian chariot culture of the Vedic type (*ratha*).

#### 3.2 Zooarchaeological Evidence: Horse Bones vs. Hemiones
A recurring argument by OIT proponents relies on alleged horse bones in mature Harappan sites such as Surkotada or Kalibangan.
- International zooarcheological reviews (Meadow & Patel) confirm: the alleged horse remains from the mature phase exclusively represent hemiones (*Equus hemionus khur*, onager) or domestic donkeys (*Equus asinus*), whose teeth and metapodials in their state of wear are often confused with true horses.
- True domesticated horse (*Equus caballus*) appears stratigraphically securely in South Asia only after 1700 BCE at sites such as Pirak (at the Bolan Pass) and in the Swat Culture (Gandhara Grave Culture) – in precise temporal coincidence with the arrival of Steppe genetics.

#### 3.3 Paleoclimatic Megadroughts and De-Urbanization
The paleoclimatic reconstruction by IIT Gandhinagar and BSIP provides the scientific explanation for the end of the urban Harappan phase:
- **Speleothem Proxies:** Oxygen isotope curves from cave speleothems in Dharamjali Cave document four centennial megadroughts, each lasting more than 85 years, between 4400 and 3400 BP.
- **Desiccation of the Ghaggar-Hakra:** Due to tectonic block tilting in the foothills and the failure of the summer monsoon, the Ghaggar-Hakra system lost its permanent glacial tributaries and transformed into ephemeral gravel beds.
- **Settlement Transformation:** Archaeological surveys show no burn or destruction horizons, but rather an ordered de-urbanization. The population abandoned the western major cities and shifted settlement centers to the more water-rich Yamuna-Ganges area (Late Harappan, Cemetery H, Painted Grey Ware).

### 4. Historical Linguistics: The Indo-European Vocabulary, Substrates, and Mitanni

#### 4.1 The Anachronism Critique of the Computational Hybrid Model
The phylolinguistic study by Heggarty et al. (Science 2023) and the subsequent discussions 2024–2026 claimed a split of Proto-Indo-European (PIE) as early as 6000 BCE in the Caucasus/Fertile Crescent region.
- **The Archaeolinguistic Contradiction:** Historical-comparative Indo-Europeanists argue that the fundamental vocabulary for wheeled vehicles is pan-Indo-European and precisely reconstructible: *kwekwlo- ("wheel"), *Hrot-o- ("wheel/cart"), *Haks- ("axle"), *yug-om ("yoke"), and *wegh- ("to ride in a cart").
- **Chronological Constraint:** Since wheeled vehicles are archaeologically attested worldwide only around 3500 BCE (Tripolye, Caucasus, Mesopotamia, Funnelbeaker culture), the fundamental dialect split of late Indo-European must have occurred after 3500 BCE. Pure Bayesian lexicostatistics without material links generates untenable deep datings.

#### 4.2 Substrate Layers in the Rigveda: Non-Indo-European Relics
The lexical analysis of Vedic Sanskrit refutes the assumption that Indo-Aryan originated autochthonously in the Indus Valley:
- Over 300 Rigvedic lexemes cannot be etymologized as Indo-European. These include central agricultural terms such as *kināśa-* ("farmer/ploughman"), *lāṅgala-* ("plough"), *ulūkhala-* ("mortar"), and river names.
- These words point to substrate languages: an early Para-Dravidian, Para-Munda, and an isolated substrate language ("Language X") associated with the original urban population of the Indus and Doab regions. In an OIT hypothesis, these expressions would be genuine basic words of Sanskrit and not morphologically divergent foreign elements.

#### 4.3 Mitanni-Aryan as a Terminus Ante Quem
The Hittite-Hurrian treaties of Boghazköy (ca. 1380 BCE) and the horse training manual of Kikkuli contain original Indo-Aryan deity and number designations:
- **Deities:** *mi-it-ra-*, *ar-u-na-*, *in-da-ra-*, *na-sa-at-ti-ya-* (Mitra, Varuna, Indra, Nasatya).
- **Horse Training:** *aika-vartana* ("one turn"), *tera-vartana* ("three turns"), *panza-vartana* ("five turns").
- **Linguistic Status:** The preservation of *aika-* (compared to Iranian *aiva-*) shows that the Indo-Aryan branch had already separated from the common Indo-Iranian base before 1400 BCE. This corresponds seamlessly with the dispersal vectors from the Andronovo/Sintashta sphere via Central Asia to West Asia and South Asia.

### 5. Indology & Text Philology: Rigvedic Geography and Dating

#### 5.1 The River Sequence of the Nadistuti-Sukta (Rigveda 10.75)
The Nadistuti-Sukta provides striking textual evidence for the geographical orientation of the Vedic poets:
- **Systematic East-West Order (RV 10.75.5):**
  *„Imam me Gaṅge Yamune Sarasvati Śutudri stomam sacatā Paruṣṇyā...“*
  (Ganges -> Yamuna -> Sarasvati -> Sutlej -> Ravi).
- **Philological Classification:** This late hymn of the tenth Mandala reflects the settlement area in the eastern Punjab and Doab. The Sarasvati is here topographically precisely located between the Yamuna and Sutlej – in the riverbed of the Ghaggar. At this late time of composition (ca. 1200–1000 BCE), the Sarasvati was no longer a huge glacial river, but a seasonally dry, sacred river, which fully aligns with the paleohydrology of the Ghaggar-Hakra.

#### 5.2 Failure of Archaeoastronomical Retro-Calculations
OIT authors (Subhash Kak, David Frawley) regularly attempt to predate the Rigveda to the 4th or even 5th millennium BCE through supposed celestial observations (such as the spring equinox in the Nakshatra Krittika/Pleiades).
- **Critique of Historical Philology:** Text critics like Michael Witzel and Stephanie Jamison have demonstrated that these passages represent purely poetic-ritual metaphors. From mythological verses, any number of spurious dates can be constructed using modern planetarium software ("astrolatry").
- **Material Culture:** The Rigveda describes a mobile, pastoral-oriented society with chariots (*ratha*), domesticated horses (*aśva*), bronze weapons (*ayas*), and cremation. This categorically excludes identification with the urban, brick-building, horseless Harappan Civilization of the 3rd millennium BCE.
