# Research Posts & Digests

Systematic evaluation, synthesis, and compression of automated literature and data surveys. "Automated" signifies that sources are selected according to predefined quality criteria, but have not undergone further manual review or verification by qualified experts.

::: details What does "OIT vs. AMT" mean? (Out of India vs. Aryan Migration Theory)
**OIT vs. AMT** compares the empirical models of the **Out of India Theory (OIT)** and the **Aryan Migration Theory (AMT / Steppe Hypothesis)**:

- **OIT (Out of India Theory):** Proposes that the Indo-European language family originated within the Indian subcontinent and subsequently expanded westward across Eurasia.
- **AMT (Aryan Migration Theory):** Represents the linguistic and archaeogenetic consensus model, positing that Indo-Iranian speakers migrated into northwestern South Asia from the Eurasian steppe via Central Asia during the 2nd millennium BCE.
:::

::: details What does "IVC" stand for? (Indus Valley Culture / Harappan Civilization)
**IVC (Indus Valley Culture / Civilization)** refers to the Bronze Age Indus Civilization (c. 3300–1300 BCE) spanning northwestern South Asia (e.g. Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira, Rakhigarhi, Lothal):

- **Focus of Monthly Reports:** Continuous synthesis of emerging archaeological excavations, radiocarbon chronologies, ancient DNA (aDNA) samplings, paleoclimatic data (sediment cores, monsoon dynamics), as well as studies on trade networks, urbanism, and deurbanization.
- **Abbreviation:** Across articles and metadata, the standard scholarly abbreviation **IVC** is uniformly used.
:::

::: details What does "Yavanas with Kharoṣṭhī Pen" mean? (Greek Elites & Gāndhārī Scribal Culture)
**Yavanas with Kharoṣṭhī Pen** investigates the intersection of Hellenistic administrative practice and Buddhist textual culture in northwestern South Asia (c. 2nd cent. BCE – 2nd cent. CE):

- **Focus of Monthly Reports:** Continuous synthesis of newly discovered epigraphic records (Kharoṣṭhī and Greek inscriptions, relic dedications, Macedonian calendar months), philological and palaeographical analyses of early Gāndhārī birch bark scrolls, and archaeological field surveys across Taxila (Sirkap) and the Swat valley.
- **Series Code:** In articles and metadata, the uniform series identifier **Yavanas** is applied.
:::
